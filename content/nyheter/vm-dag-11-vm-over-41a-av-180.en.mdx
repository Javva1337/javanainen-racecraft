My World Championship is over. After missing out on the final, the semifinal turned into a race in a kart that never gave me a chance to fight for positions. I leave Denmark 41st of 180 drivers — and, despite everything, with the feeling that I can still compete up there.

## The match race: drawn against the best right away

The day began with the head-to-head match race qualifying, where drivers go up against each other one on one. I was drawn against Ruben Boutens in the very first round — a driver I would rank as one of the best, maybe the best, rental kart driver of all time. Going out against him is nothing to be ashamed of.

The loss came at a price, though. Ruben got to choose his kart for the semifinal, and I was assigned kart number 40. I had neither seen nor driven it during the week, and within the first few laps it was clear the pace simply wasn't there.

## The semifinal: a lonely race in kart 40

I started 15th of 18 drivers. Within a couple of corners I was last, and from there I drove almost the entire 30-minute race on my own, with no chance to fight for positions.

The numbers tell most of the story: my fastest lap was three tenths slower than the second-slowest driver in the field, and a full eight tenths off the fastest. For parts of the race I was also close to being lapped by the winner.

Frustrating, of course. At the same time I knew the final was already out of reach, so whether I finished a few places higher or lower no longer made much difference.

## The feeling after the Worlds

I finish this World Championship in 41st place of 180 drivers. Considering how little I have driven ahead of this year's championship, I'm actually pleased with that. I drive maybe five to eight times a year in total, while many of my competitors fit that much into one or two weeks. For them, this is a full-time job, with training and racing all over the world.

Apart from the local drivers, who already know the track, I also had the least practice of almost anyone at the event. Better preparation would have made a real difference and helped me find my pace earlier in the week.

I have reached the Worlds final every previous year I've entered. This time it wasn't enough. I can accept that — and at the same time it motivates me for the future.

## What I take with me

- Better preparation before big championships makes a real difference.
- Even with limited seat time, it's possible to compete near the top of the world field.
- Sometimes the equipment decides more than you'd like. Then you have to accept it and move on.
- A big thank-you to Primab and Labatus — their support made this Worlds campaign possible.
- And thank you to everyone who followed the race reports, cheered and sent messages during the week. It means more than you know.
